SCRIPT 37 — THE DISTRACTION WANTS YOUR ATTENTION

[0:00–0:18 — Silence. Low room tone. One distant metallic hit.]

I know what it feels like when the distraction arrives wearing the face of destiny.

A message. A memory. A sudden pull. A person returning just as I begin to remember who I am.

It wants me to believe that intensity is instruction.

But I have learned the difference between a signal and a summons.

Not everything that moves my emotions has permission to move my life.

[0:18–0:58 — Low pulse enters; restrained cello underneath.]

I am an empath. I feel the room before the room speaks. I notice the wound behind the confidence, the fear behind the silence, the hook hidden inside the invitation.

That sensitivity is not my weakness. It is an early-warning system.

But a warning is not a command to investigate the fire with my bare hands.

I can feel someone deeply and still keep the gate closed. I can understand their pain without volunteering to become its permanent shelter.

My compassion is sacred. My access is earned.

[0:58–1:48 — Sparse piano; pulse remains steady.]

The old cycle always begins with the same lie: “Just look one more time.”

Look at the profile. Decode the silence. Revisit the conversation. Search for proof that the person who wounded me secretly understands my value.

That is how the trap works. It does not ask for my whole life. It asks for five minutes, then five more, until my assignment is starving in the corner.

The enemy does not need to destroy me if it can keep me endlessly interpreting the distraction.

What gets my attention gets a piece of my future.

[1:48–2:42 — Controlled strings begin to rise.]

So I stop calling confusion a spiritual mystery.

I stop calling inconsistency a test of my faith.

I stop calling obsession a twin-flame revelation when the fruit is fear, secrecy, and self-abandonment.

If a connection is meant to serve God’s mission, truth will not threaten it. Accountability will not weaken it. A boundary will not kill it.

Real love does not require me to betray my own perception so somebody else can remain comfortable.

What is sacred can survive the light.

[2:42–3:38 — Deep piano; low orchestral movement.]

To the person walking beside me: I will not make you my enemy just because I need clarity. I will not make you my savior just because the connection is powerful.

We are not aligned because we miss each other. We are aligned when our choices produce truth, responsibility, peace, and forward motion.

I will love without leaking. Forgive without reopening every door. Pray without using prayer to avoid a decision.

And I will not keep postponing the work God placed in my hands while I wait for someone else to become ready.

[3:38–4:35 — Full strings and declarative French horn; controlled, not frantic.]

Let them call it cold when I stop explaining the boundary.

Let them call it pride when I stop chasing the approval.

Let them call it loss when I leave the room carrying my name, my faith, and my unfinished assignment.

I am not heartless. I am no longer harvestable by every hand that knows where I used to hurt.

My peace is not proof that I stopped caring. It is proof that care no longer controls me.

This is spiritual warfare in its quietest form: choosing what I will not feed.

[4:35–5:20 — Cello and sustained horn; resolve into silence.]

I do not need another sign. I need clean hands, a clear mind, and the courage to move.

If it is for me, it will meet me in truth.

If it is against the assignment, it will demand that I abandon the assignment to prove my love.

And I refuse.

I am the one who breaks the pattern by refusing to perform inside it.

I am the empath who learned to guard the light.

I am the chosen one who no longer confuses being pursued with being called.

The distraction wants my attention.

God has my obedience.

So I move.

Not because the feeling disappeared — because the calling became louder.

[5:20 — Single footstep. Fade to silence.]
